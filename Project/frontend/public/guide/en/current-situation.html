Current situation

A "cut" is a point-in-time snapshot of every bank account's balance, saved against a specific date. Cuts are what powers the dashboard's history and let you compare your actual position to what the budget expected. This chapter covers navigating between cuts, entering balances, saving, reading the totals, and deleting a cut record.

Navigating between cut dates

The date navigator moves one cut at a time, forward or backward, jumping only between dates that already have a saved record. Picking a date with no existing record opens a prompt asking how to seed it: start every account at 0 ("Start blank"), copy the balances from the most recent saved cut ("Clone from last record"), or copy from a specific earlier date you choose from a list. Until you save, the new date is a Draft.

Entering balances and the exchange rate

Accounts are split into two groups matching their type from the Bank accounts chapter: assets that add to the total, and liabilities that subtract from it. Each row takes the account's balance as of the cut date; the record also carries a single exchange rate used to convert any alternate-currency accounts into the budget's primary currency for the totals below.

Saving a cut

Only operators, admins, and the owner can save. Saving requires an active budget period that covers the cut date — if none exists yet, the save is rejected rather than silently accepted against nothing, since a cut with no matching period would have no execution data to compare against.

Reading the totals and execution summary

The totals panel adds up assets, liabilities, and net position at the cut's exchange rate, alongside a budget execution summary — total budgeted, total registered, and the remaining commitment — for the period the cut date falls in. These figures are a snapshot: they reflect account balances and execution as of the cut's last save, so later changes to accounts or execution records don't move a saved cut's totals until you save it again.

Deleting a cut record

Deleting a cut is permanent, so the confirmation asks you to type the exact cut date before the delete button becomes clickable — a plain "confirm" click isn't enough for an irreversible action. Once deleted, the view falls back to the most recent remaining cut, if any.
