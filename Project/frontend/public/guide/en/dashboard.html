Dashboard

The dashboard turns your saved cuts and execution history into charts. It has three self-contained sections on one page, no tabs to switch between: a lifetime trend, an average behavior band, and a budget-line comparison with two modes of its own. This chapter covers all three, plus the currency-mismatch guard and how the layout adapts on narrow screens.

Lifetime trend

This is the dashboard's default view — the one you see first, before any average or comparison chart. It plots every series you pick (Total Assets, Total Liabilities, Total Debt, Total Available, Total Net, and their budgeted/registered counterparts) across every saved cut, oldest to newest. A picker to the side lets you select or clear individual series, or all of them at once.

Average behavior band

Below the lifetime trend, the average behavior chart shows how each series tends to move across periods rather than across raw cut dates. It needs at least two periods with recorded cuts to mean anything — with fewer, the dashboard shows an explicit "not enough history yet" message instead of drawing a misleading band from a single data point.

Budget line behavior — within a cycle

The third section compares specific budget lines instead of the account-level totals above. Pick one or more lines from the line picker, then choose periods to compare. In "Within cycle" mode you first pick a Cycle and then two or more of its Periods; the chart stays empty until both a line and at least one period are selected.

Budget line behavior — across cycles

Switching the mode to "Cross-cycle" replaces the single-cycle period picker with a list of Cycles — pick two or more, and every period of each selected cycle is included in the comparison. Switching modes clears whatever was selected in the other mode, so a leftover within-cycle pick never gets mixed into a cross-cycle chart or vice versa.

Currency mismatch guard

Every line you pick belongs to a Cycle, and every Cycle has a currency. If the periods selected for comparison span cycles with different currencies, the chart refuses to render at all — instead of silently converting or blending two currencies onto one axis, it shows a warning explaining that those lines cannot be compared together. This mirrors a similar rule elsewhere in the app: mismatched currencies are never combined into one figure.

One more subtlety worth knowing: the lifetime trend and average behavior charts convert alternate-currency amounts at each cut's own frozen exchange rate, while the budget-line chart converts at the exchange rate in effect when each transaction happened. The two charts can therefore show slightly different converted totals for the same underlying data — this is expected, not a bug.

Responsive layout

On a narrow screen, the charts and their side pickers stack vertically instead of sitting side by side, and nothing scrolls off the edge of the viewport.
